## 3. Decisions so far (all still in force)

### Structure
1. **Hierarchy: Product > Capability > Feature > Story.** Sponsors accepted it.
   - A **capability** is a part of a product a customer can turn on or off, for example Fax Intake, SFTP Intake, API Intake, or Mailroom Intake.
   - Every capability has at least one feature, for example Dedicated Fax Lines under Fax Intake.
2. **Appeals is a workflow,** not a product. It uses two products (which two is still open).
3. **SDS taxonomy spreadsheet** (Solution, Product Family, Product, Capability, Service, Product Enhancement) is **reference vocabulary only.** No restructuring.
4. **Customer settings are copied into YAML** in the docs, using safe-copy rules. Record the source (file path, or table and column) of every copied value. SDS may later make QuickClaim read these files.
5. **Environments:** local, test, prod. No QA yet.
6. **Scope:** CENTENE only. Clearinghouse does not need review.

### Change requests (Zach's top priority, 10/27)
- A customer change request (CR) can become: something only for that customer, an enhancement, or a product change. Track both the CR and what it became.
- The CR is its own document in the client folder: `CRID-CENCORP-003`. It records what was asked, a required **Reason**, the outcome type, and everything it created.
- Everything a CR created carries `Change request: CRID-...`.
- Items from a **customer-only** CR also carry `Client:` and are marked **Contractual: yes**, so people and AI agents do not change or delete them freely. Enhancements and product changes never carry `Client:`.
- Every change has an audit reason. SDS is audited often.
- **Termination test:** if a customer leaves, an agent (even a cheap model) follows the links from the client folder, finds everything tied only to that customer, skips anything other customers use, and lists what can be removed. This is the main Goal 2 proof, run in week 3.
- CENCORP may have no formal CRs yet. Implementation-specific needs still count. Zach's example: CENCORP has documents that must be labeled a certain way for its workflow only. Document that as a CR or variation.

### Customer differences
- Not every difference is a CR. Many are config or code overrides of the base customer BCS. These are **variations**: `VRID-CENCORP-002`. A variation always carries `Client:` and points to the feature it changes, plus the CR that caused it if there is one.
- Six variation types: config, database, code override, hardcoded branch, custom template, custom package.
